The Fivefold Journey

From Nature To Your Kitchen

Every single bottle undergoes five slow, deliberate steps of craftsmanship — honoring centuries of Indian wood-press heritage.

01

Carefully Selected

We partner directly with heritage farming families. Only sun-dried Malabar coconuts, Saurashtra heirloom groundnuts, and wild black sesame seeds pass our moisture and density inspection.

Standard: A+ Grade Only Rejection Rate: 18% Filtered Out
02

Naturally Prepared

Fresh coconut copra and seeds are hand-sliced and sun-cured over organic reed mats in coastal courtyards. Zero sulphur bleaching, zero chemical fumigation, zero synthetic drying ovens.

Curing: Sun & Wind Only Moisture Target: 4.5% to 5.0%
03

Traditionally Pressed

Cold-pressed inside ancient Vaagai (Albizia Lebbeck) hardwood mortars. The heavy pestle turns at under 14 RPM, keeping temperatures strictly below 45°C to preserve active enzymes and antioxidants.

Friction Heat: < 38.5°C Measured Timber: Aged Vaagai Hardwood
04

Naturally Filtered

Instead of chemical solvent clarification or high-pressure filters, our raw oil settles naturally in earthenware vats for 72 hours, then gravity-drips through 4 layers of organic unbleached cotton muslin.

Clarification: 72h Gravity Settling Filter Media: Organic Raw Muslin
05

Purely Bottled

Every bottle of The Rudrayya Oil Mill is filled by hand, sealed with natural cork stoppers, tied with organic jute cord, and stamped with our single-estate harvest batch certificate.

Packaging: Apothecary Glass Seal: Natural Biodegradable Cork

Generational Philosophy

More Than Oil.
A Tradition.

“True cooking oil is not an industrial solvent. It is the living essence of the soil, unlocked only through unhurried patience.”

At The Rudrayya Oil Mill, our founders rebelled against modern commercial refining: the high-heat deodorization, chemical hexane washing, and synthetic bleaches that stripped oils of their natural aroma, color, and soul.

Instead, we restored the ancient South Indian tradition of the Marachekku. We harvest when the sun is right, cure on reed mats in village courtyards, and cold-press using aged Vaagai wood at under 14 revolutions per minute.
